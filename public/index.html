Calendly Labs · AI Assistant case

Never say “I’ll get back to you” again.

Callie Live Assistant is a private assistant for customer calls. It answers the customer’s question before you finish saying “good question,” catches your mistakes before they build on them, and puts the right diagram or doc on screen while you’re still talking.

    Priority 3

    Coaching

    Callie reads the customer’s tone from her voice and words. When she sounds confused, skeptical or frustrated, or when the CSM has talked too long, a private nudge suggests the next move. It’s rate-limited, and it’s never visible to the customer or reported to a manager.

    Integration and setup

    Set up once by an admin. Nothing to do before each call.

    Callie Live Assistant is a desktop app that signs in with Calendly. It hears the call at the computer level, so it works next to Zoom, Meet or Teams without a bot.

    1. 1

      Install and sign in

      Mac or Windows app, Calendly single sign-on. The admin turns it on for the CS team in Calendly’s admin settings.

    2. 2

      Connect the knowledge

      Point it at the docs site, help center, and Notion, Confluence or Google Drive. Callie indexes them and refreshes nightly.

    3. 3

      Review the key facts

      An admin confirms a short list of facts that must never be wrong: plans, limits, compliance. Fact-checks lean on these first.

    4. 4

      Connect email

      Gmail or Outlook, so “Send” goes out from the CSM’s own address after their click.

    5. 5

      Booking context flows in

      Event type, invitee answers, notes and earlier Notetaker recaps arrive with each call. Nothing to paste.

    6. 6

      Consent built in

      A disclosure line in the booking confirmation, no audio stored by default, and admin controls for retention and who can turn it on.

    Quality

    Judged feature by feature, before launch and every week after.

    A confidently wrong answer or a wrong correction is a serious error (a “Sev-1”). Launch requires none in the last 200 test questions.

    FeatureWhat good looks likeHow we measure itLaunch gate
    Answers: say itCorrect, grounded, short enough to sayPrecision on a gold set of 300 real call questions; citation coverage; in-call resolutionZero Sev-1; p50 under 3 s, p90 under 5 s
    Answers: show itThe visual matches what the CSM is explainingWeekly taste review; how often visuals get presentedNo invented package names or limits
    Answers: send itThe customer can follow it aloneShare of drafts sent with light or no editsEvery step and link traceable to a source

    Every call line is a test case

    Each scripted line has an expected outcome: answer, correction, clarify or coach. The suite reruns on every prompt or model change. This prototype’s recorded call has 15 checks and all of them pass, and the live call has its own end-to-end test with a simulated microphone.

    A weekly taste review

    Design and engineering read 30 real cards together each week. Was it right, was it needed, was it on time, was it kind? The answers tune prompts and interruption thresholds.

    From now to launch

    Twelve weeks, four checkpoints.

    Each phase ends with a plain test. We only move on when it passes.

    1. 1Weeks 0–2

      Move on whenthree design partners have signed, we have 300 real questions to test against, and we agree on what counts as a serious mistake.
    2. 2Weeks 3–6

      Build in a weekly loop

      Move on whenit answers at least 8 of 10 test questions correctly with a source, and Calendly’s own CSMs keep it on for a full week.
    3. 3Weeks 7–9

    4. 4Weeks 10–12

      Public beta

      Open to Calendly Teams customers as a paid add-on (Teams Plus), once it passes the three launch gates below.

      Launch whenall three gates pass.

    The three launch gates, in plain English

    1No confident mistakes

    In the last 200 test questions, Callie never gives a confidently wrong answer or a wrong correction. Engineers call this “zero Sev-1.”

    2Fast enough to use mid-sentence

    9 out of 10 cards show up within 5 seconds of the question. Engineers call this “p90 under 5 seconds.”

    3CSMs agree with its corrections

    CSMs accept at least 8 out of 10 corrections instead of dismissing them.

    What hears the call

    No bot joins the meeting.

    Callie listens on your side of the call, the same way your own ears do. Here’s where the sound comes from in the real product, and in this demo.

    The real product
    1. Your Zoom, Meet or Teams callNothing changes for the customer. No extra participant, no recording bot.
    2. Your laptop’s soundThe customer’s voice coming out of your speakers, and your microphone.
    3. Callie Live Assistant, a desktop appHears both sides on your computer and keeps them apart, so it knows who said what.
    4. A private panel only you seeAnswers, fact-checks and coaching next to your call.
    This demo
    1. A simulated video callGrace (the customer) and Julian (an Arize engineer) are AI, on a real video-call service.
    2. Your browser taps the call’s audioIt passes their voices, and your microphone or typed lines, to Callie. This stands in for the desktop app.
    3. The Callie serverDoes exactly what the product would: listens, looks things up, thinks, and responds.
    4. The panel on the rightThe same cards, whiteboard and email drafts.

    What happens to each thing someone says

    Hear, understand, look it up, respond.

    A few seconds from the end of a question to a card on your screen: about five to eight in this prototype.

    1. 1HearVoice activity detection

      Splits the call into short pieces of speech, each tagged with who spoke: you, the customer or your teammate.

    2. 2UnderstandGemini 3.8 Flash

      A fast model writes down what was said and decides what kind of moment it is: a question, a claim worth checking, a request to Callie, or just conversation. It also notes the customer’s tone. Takes about two seconds, and it hears product names right.

    3. 3Look it upKeyword + meaning search

      Finds the few passages that matter in the company’s docs (892 Arize passages here) and a short list of facts that must never be wrong.

    4. 4ThinkGemini 3.8 Flash, two requests race

      The same model writes the answer, the correction or the coaching tip, always with its source. Two requests run at once and the first good answer wins, because speed varies from call to call.

    5. 5RespondThe panel

      One card in focus, a drawing on the whiteboard, or an email draft that waits for your click. Optionally, a short whisper in your ear (Gemini 3.8 Live).

    Deep dive: finding the right page

    Keyword search plus Gemini embeddings.

    Before Callie answers, it finds the few passages in the company’s docs that actually answer the question. Two searches run at once, because each one catches what the other misses.

    Before the call · indexing the docs
    • CrawlThe docs site, help center and wikis. Here: 65 Arize pages.
    • Split by headingEach section becomes a passage: 892 in this prototype.
    • Keyword indexCounts which words each passage uses (BM25), with headings counted twice because they say what a section is about.
    • Meaning indexGemini Embedding 2 turns every passage into 768 numbers, stored next to the text.
    • Key factsA short list an admin verifies (plans, limits, retention). Answers and fact-checks lean on it first.
    During the call · answering a question
    • Both searches at onceKeyword scores and meaning scores are each scaled from 0 to 1, then blended 40 / 60.
    • Six passages, at most two per pageSo one long page can’t crowd out the rest.
    • Grounded answerThe model may use only those passages and the key facts, and says when it isn’t sure.
    • Sources on every replyThe pages it used become the links under Callie’s answer.
    • Kept freshThe index refreshes nightly; questions it couldn’t answer become a docs-gap list.
